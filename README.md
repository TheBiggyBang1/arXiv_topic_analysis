## Problem

Thousands of papers appear on arXiv every week. We use LDA to discover, without labels, the
research topics present in a corpus of computer-science abstracts, describe each paper as a
mixture of topics, and check how well the discovered topics match the official arXiv categories.

## Dataset

| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Source | arXiv API (<https://info.arxiv.org/help/api/index.html>), via the `arxiv` Python package |
| Categories | cs.CL, cs.CV, cs.RO, cs.CR, cs.DB, cs.NI (primary category only) |
| Period | 2025-01-01 → 2025-06-30, earliest submissions first |
| Size | 10,197 papers (cs.CV 2307 · cs.RO 2228 · cs.CL 2185 · cs.CR 2141 · cs.NI 874 · cs.DB 462), ~176 words per abstract |
| Language | English |
| Fields | `id`, `title`, `abstract`, `category`, `published` |
| Labels | `category` is **not** used for training — only to evaluate topics afterwards |

The downloaded file is saved as `data/arxiv_abstracts.csv`. Thank you to arXiv for use of its
open access interoperability.

## Results

| K | Coherence C_v (mean ± std, 3 seeds) | Topic diversity |
|---|---|---|
| **5** | **0.54 ± 0.04** | **0.95** |
| 10 | 0.47 ± 0.02 | 0.88 |
| 15 | 0.47 ± 0.02 | 0.87 |

**Selected K = 5** — topics: Robotics & autonomy · LLMs & language · Networking · Computer vision · Security & privacy.
Four topics match an arXiv category by their words alone. No topic emerges for cs.DB, the smallest category.
K = 10 adds interdisciplinary topics (vision-language models, network/IoT security, LLM attacks), but about 3 of
its 10 topics are noise.

**Document-topic analysis (K = 5):**
- Most papers mix topics: ~5,200 of 10,197 have two topics with θ ≥ 0.2. 20 % have two topics ≥ 0.35
  (e.g. language-guided robots, LLM security, video LLMs).
- Dominant topic vs arXiv category: **NMI = 0.51, ARI = 0.52**, and 77 % of papers fall into their category's
  expected topic (cs.CL 85 %, cs.CV 83 %, cs.RO 80 %, cs.NI 78 %, cs.CR 68 %, cs.DB 43 %).
- Error analysis: many "disagreements" are genuinely interdisciplinary papers. Real errors come from shared
  vocabulary (e.g. BERT pruning → Vision).
- Interactive topic map: `figures/ldavis_k5.html` (open in a browser).

## Limitations

- **Unbalanced data:** cs.DB (4.5 % of papers) gets no topic of its own and splits across three topics.
- **Snapshot, not a timeline:** busy categories cover only weeks of early 2025, so topics reflect current
  (LLM-heavy) research and say nothing about trends.
- **Easier than real arXiv:** six distinct fields were hand-picked and cs.LG excluded. Abstracts only, no full text.
- **Bag-of-words:** papers sharing vocabulary but not purpose get confused (e.g. BERT pruning → Vision).
- **Subjective choices:** part of the stop-word list was tuned after reading topics (C_v 0.48 → 0.54), K was chosen
  among only 5/10/15, results vary across seeds (C_v 0.50–0.57 at K = 5), and topic names are our interpretation.
- **Imperfect evaluation:** coherence is a proxy, and single-label arXiv categories make NMI/ARI penalise
  legitimately interdisciplinary papers.

**Improvements:** balanced multi-year sample, finer K sweep with more seeds, comparison with an embedding-based
topic model (BERTopic). Details in notebook section 6.

## Conclusion

Without labels, LDA recovered the main research fields of 10,197 arXiv abstracts (4 of 5 topics match a category,
NMI 0.51). It also showed that much current CS research, often driven by LLMs, sits *between* fields:
about half of the papers mix two topics.
